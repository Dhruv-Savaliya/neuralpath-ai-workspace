# Market Structures and Competition

Economists categorize industries based on the degree of competition among participating firms, the barriers to market entry, and the uniqueness of products. The four traditional market structures are perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, and pure monopoly.

In perfect competition, numerous small buyers and sellers exchange homogeneous commodities, resulting in zero pricing power where each firm acts as a price taker. In monopolistic competition, many producers sell differentiated products, providing moderate pricing discretion through brand identity and design variation.

An oligopoly features a small number of dominant firms whose strategic decisions directly affect one another, often leading to non-price competition or game-theoretic collusion. Finally, a pure monopoly exists when a single seller controls the entire supply of a good with significant barriers preventing new competitors from entering.
