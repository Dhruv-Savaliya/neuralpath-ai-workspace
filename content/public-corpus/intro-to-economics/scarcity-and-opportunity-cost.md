# Scarcity, Choice, and Opportunity Cost

Economics is the study of how individuals, businesses, and governments allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited human needs and desires. The condition of scarcity arises because physical resources—including land, labor, capital, and raw materials—exist in finite supply relative to societal demands.

Because of scarcity, decision-makers face continuous tradeoffs. Whenever a choice is made, an alternative option is forgone. The opportunity cost of a decision represents the value of the next best alternative given up in making that choice. Opportunity cost encompasses not only monetary expenses, but also time, effort, and forgone utility.

The production possibilities frontier (PPF) is an economic model illustrating the maximum combinations of two goods or services an economy can produce efficiently given fixed technology and inputs. Operating inside the curve indicates underutilized resources, while points on the frontier reflect productive efficiency.
