# Supply, Demand, and Market Equilibrium

The model of supply and demand describes how prices emerge and guide resource allocation in market economies. The law of demand states that, all else being equal, as the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded falls, generating a downward-sloping demand curve.

Conversely, the law of supply posits that as the market price of a product increases, producers are incentivized to supply a greater quantity, producing an upward-sloping supply curve. Factors that shift demand or supply include consumer preferences, input costs, technological advancements, taxes, and substitute availability.

Market equilibrium occurs at the intersection of the supply and demand curves, where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied ($Q_d = Q_s$). At this equilibrium price, the market clears without persistent shortages or surpluses. Competitive price mechanisms dynamically adjust to re-establish balance whenever shocks alter economic conditions.
