# Systems of Linear Equations

A system of linear equations consists of two or more linear equations sharing the same set of unknown variables. Finding a solution to a system means identifying values for the variables that simultaneously satisfy every equation in the system.

In two dimensions, the solution corresponds to the point of intersection between two straight lines. If the lines intersect at exactly one point, the system is consistent and independent. If the lines are parallel and distinct, no solution exists (an inconsistent system). If the lines coincide, infinitely many solutions exist along the line (a dependent system).

The three primary algebraic techniques for solving linear systems are graphing, substitution, and elimination. The elimination method involves adding or subtracting scalar multiples of the equations to cancel out one variable, making it particularly effective for systems with higher numbers of variables.
