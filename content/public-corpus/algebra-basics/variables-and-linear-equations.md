# Variables and Linear Equations

Algebra introduces symbolic variables to represent unknown numerical values, allowing mathematical relationships to be stated generally and solved systematically. In an algebraic expression, letters such as $x$ and $y$ represent quantities that can vary or that we seek to determine.

A linear equation in one variable takes the standard form $ax + b = 0$, where $a$ and $b$ are real numbers and $a \neq 0$. The fundamental principle of solving linear equations relies on maintaining equality: performing equivalent operations on both sides of the equation, including addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division by non-zero quantities.

Graphically, a linear equation in two variables $y = mx + b$ represents a straight line in the Cartesian coordinate plane. The slope $m$ measures the steepness and direction of the line, defined as the ratio of vertical change to horizontal change ($\Delta y / \Delta x$), while $b$ represents the $y$-intercept where the line crosses the vertical axis.
