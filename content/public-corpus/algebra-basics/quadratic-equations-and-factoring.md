# Quadratic Equations and Factoring

A quadratic equation is a second-degree polynomial equation in a single variable $x$, written in standard form as $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where $a, b, c$ are real constants and $a \neq 0$. The graph of a quadratic function is a symmetric curve known as a parabola.

Quadratic equations can be solved using multiple methods: factoring, completing the square, or applying the quadratic formula:
$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

The discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ reveals the nature and count of the roots without fully solving the equation. If $\Delta > 0$, the equation possesses two distinct real roots; if $\Delta = 0$, exactly one real repeated root exists; and if $\Delta < 0$, the equation yields two complex conjugate roots. Factoring polynomials allows algebraic expressions to be decomposed into products of linear factors.
